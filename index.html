Hardware connection

The layer that makes our physical AI different, also available on its own for your robot and your AI stack. It connects to the boards, motors and sensors directly.

Bring-up without the integration project

Most robot AI sits on top of an integration layer that engineers write by hand for every new vehicle. Our AI is connected to the hardware itself. Give it a new motor controller, camera or IMU and it reads the datasheet, writes the driver, tests it on the bench, runs identification to learn how the machine moves, and finds faults in the raw signals.

Use it under our physical AI, or plug it under your own model, ROS 2 stack or flight controller.

Builds the drivers

Give it a new motor controller, camera or IMU and it writes the driver, tests it on the bench and keeps it.

  • Reads datasheets and protocols
  • Writes, compiles and tests driver code
  • Works with common boards and buses

Identifies the system

It runs its own experiments to learn mass, inertia, motor response and delays, then builds a model it can control.

  • Automatic test signals and data capture
  • Model fitting and validation
  • Controller tuning from the fitted model

Debugs itself

When a sensor drifts, a motor stalls or a cable is wrong, the AI sees it in the raw data and works out the cause.

  • Fault detection from raw signals
  • Root cause with a plain explanation
  • Safe fallback while it fixes the problem

How a project runs

Bring your vehicle, or let us build one. The AI does the bring-up, and your team watches every step.

Connect

We plug the AI into your robot's boards, motors and sensors, with no custom layer in between.

Bring-up

Field test
